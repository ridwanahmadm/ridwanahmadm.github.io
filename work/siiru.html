Siiru: one app for every step to Umrah

I design Siiru, a companion app for Muslims in Malaysia, Indonesia and Singapore. It brings 17 services together, from daily prayer and giving to booking a guide, a ride or travel insurance.

My part: I designed most of the app's core flows and own its final visual style and design decisions, across app and web. I also made the product film, mentor a junior designer and run design critiques.

The problem: Umrah planning is scattered

Around 1.7 million Muslims from Southeast Asia travel to Makkah every year. Planning a self-organised Umrah can take 40 to 60 hours across five or more vendors for visas, hotels, transport, guides and insurance, and none of them stays with the pilgrim before, during and after the trip.

Siiru wants to be the one companion that does. My job is to make seventeen services feel like one calm product.

Most users are not travelling this year

Most people who open an Umrah app are not flying soon. Their connection to Makkah is daily: prayer, giving, and sending a dua with a friend who is going. So we shaped the app around four modes instead of one booking funnel.

Always
Prayer times, giving and dua for everyone
Before
Planning, saving and learning
During
Guides, rides and worship in Makkah and Madinah
After
Returning the duas people carried, and giving again

An app people already open every day is trusted by the time they plan a trip. That is why daily features lead the home screen.

Making guide booking feel safe

Booking a mutawwif means trusting a stranger with the most important days of your year. If comparing two guides means opening two profiles, people hesitate or leave the app to ask around on WhatsApp.

I put the trust signals people ask about on the card itself and kept Quick Book there too. To test it, I prepared a think-aloud usability study that covers finding the feature, choosing a guide, scheduling and checkout, scored with the System Usability Scale.

Designing AI that people trust

Plan Your Trip turns a few answers into a complete Umrah itinerary. Two decisions shaped it. There is no empty prompt box, because most people have never planned a trip like this and do not know what to ask. And while the AI works, it shows its steps in plain words, such as finding places in Madinah and fitting prayer times, so the plan feels considered rather than generated.

The product film

I directed and built a two-minute film in Remotion, so the video is a React project. One project renders five versions, each with its own language, currency and voice-over. Prices inside the recorded app screens are found with OCR and rewritten in RM or S$, and an automated check reviews all 19 scenes in every version before export.

Beyond the app

The same principles carry into Siiru's web presence. With AI as a pair programmer, I designed and built a new company profile and a public report that shows donors where their gifts went.

How I work with the team

Every new design or iteration at Siiru goes through critique with developers, the PM and the leadership team. Critique is where competing priorities become shared decisions; the booking flow is one design that changed because of it.

I also mentor a junior designer through weekly one-on-ones, tasks aimed at specific gaps, and a monthly team checkpoint that shows each designer their progress and pace.

What I am learning

Seventeen services can easily become seventeen competing priorities. The work that matters most is deciding what each person needs right now, and hiding the rest.
