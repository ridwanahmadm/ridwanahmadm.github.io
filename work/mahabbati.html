Mahabbati: a ta'aruf app with an AI matchmaker

Mahabbati helps Muslims find a spouse through ta'aruf, the Islamic way of getting to know someone for marriage, and then prepare for the wedding itself. Its flagship is an AI matchmaker that turns a few words about the person you hope for into ranked, verified matches.

My part: I designed the whole app as a freelance product designer in 2026, from the AI matchmaker to verification, membership, pre-marriage learning and the wedding organizer. I also directed and built its company profile film and vertical promo.

The problem: ta'aruf left hanging

Ta'aruf is meant to be calm and intentional. On many apps, a request is read and never answered, the quota it used is gone, and there is no easy way to tell whether a profile is real.

Mahabbati's own Google Play reviews and Instagram comments raised the same worries: paying for requests that go nowhere, and how to know a candidate is genuine. The design answers each of them.

The flagship: an AI matchmaker

Instead of a long filter form, people describe who they hope for in their own words, such as "patient and prays five times a day". The AI suggests criteria to refine the search and returns ranked recommendations, each with a match percentage.

On a candidate's page, the score never stands alone. It is labelled with what it means and what it is based on: verified profiles and sharia-aligned criteria. A high number invites a closer look; it does not make the decision.

Ta'aruf with a deadline

Every request has a clear deadline. If there is no reply within seven days, it is cancelled automatically and the quota comes back. The Ta'arufku screen shows how much time is left on each request, so nobody waits without knowing.

Trust and privacy, by design

Every user goes through the same check, local or international: an e-KTP for Indonesians or a passport for everyone else, plus a selfie. Women's photos are reviewed only by female admins. Candidate cards use illustrated avatars, and a real photo opens for 24 hours only after a request is accepted, without a download option.

From introduction to walimah

Mahabbati keeps going after the match. Members prepare through classes and a pre-marriage exam with a certificate, keep up with daily duas and prayer times, and can book a sharia wedding organizer in the same app. Membership tiers decide how many requests and benefits each member gets.

The films

To introduce the app, I directed and built an 88-second company profile in Remotion, in Indonesian, English and Arabic, which reads right to left. A 45-second vertical promo for Reels and TikTok follows a problem, agitation and solution arc taken from real reviews: a request left on read, a quota lost, then the quota coming back.

Beyond the app
